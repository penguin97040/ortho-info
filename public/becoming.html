Becoming Orthodox

In short There’s no rush. Joining the Orthodox Church usually takes several months to a year or more, and your priest guides you the whole way.

The usual path

  1. Keep coming

    Go to the Liturgy regularly. If you can, try Vespers on Saturday evening too. Watch, listen and let it sink in.

  2. Talk to the priest

    Tell him you’re interested. Priests are used to this, and it won’t be awkward. He’ll probably suggest reading and regular chats.

  3. Become a catechumen

    A short service formally enrols you as a learner. You’re now part of the community, though you don’t receive Communion yet.

  4. Learn

    Classes or one-on-one time covering the Creed, prayer, the sacraments and church life. Start a simple daily prayer rule as well.

  5. Choose a saint and a sponsor

    You take the name of a patron saint. A sponsor (godparent; kum or kuma in Serbian) is an Orthodox Christian who walks with you.

  6. First confession

    Usually just before you’re received. It’s a fresh start, not an interrogation, and the priest will help you.

  7. Be received

    Either by baptism and chrismation (anointing with holy oil, “the seal of the gift of the Holy Spirit”), or by chrismation alone if you were already baptised in the name of the Trinity. Practice varies by bishop. Your priest will explain what applies to you.

  8. First Communion

    Usually at the same Liturgy. Then the real journey starts: a lifelong rhythm of prayer, confession, fasting and Communion.

Quick questions

Does my Protestant faith “not count”?

Your love for Christ and the Bible is real, and you bring it with you. Converts often describe joining as finding the fullness of what they already had, not starting over.

Will I need to be baptised again?

It depends on the bishop and the parish. Some receive baptised Christians by chrismation, others baptise all converts. Ask your priest rather than the internet.

Do I need to be Serbian, or learn Serbian?

No. Picking up a few phrases is a nice gesture, but the faith is the same in any language.

Can I take Communion before I join?

No. Wait until you’re received. You can still receive blessed bread at the end of the Liturgy and ask the priest for a blessing.

What about my family?

Many converts have Protestant or non-religious families. Go gently. You don’t need to win any arguments. Living it out is the best explanation.

Things you can start now

  • Morning and evening prayers: five minutes from an Orthodox prayer book.
  • A prayer corner: one icon on a shelf, maybe a candle.
  • The Jesus Prayer: “Lord Jesus Christ, Son of God, have mercy on me, a sinner.” Say it anywhere, anytime.
  • Read the Gospels, slowly, a chapter at a time.
