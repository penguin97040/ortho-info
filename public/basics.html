The big picture

In short Orthodoxy is the Church of the first Christian centuries, still worshipping and believing the way it did then. You already share more with it than you might think.

Orthodoxy and Protestantism have more in common than you think

Both agree on the big things:

  • One God: Father, Son and Holy Spirit.
  • Jesus is fully God and fully human. He died and rose from the dead.
  • The Bible is God’s word.
  • Salvation is a gift of God’s grace.
  • The Nicene Creed, which many Protestant churches say too.

The differences are mostly about how these are lived out, and who has the authority to interpret them.

Where it came from

  1. About AD 33The Church begins at Pentecost. The apostles spread out across the Roman Empire and beyond.
  2. 325–787Seven councils of the whole Church settle core beliefs, like the Trinity and who Jesus is. The Nicene Creed comes from here.
  3. 1054The Great Schism. The Church in the West (Rome) and the East split, mainly over the pope’s authority and a change to the Creed.
  4. 1517The Reformation splits the Western Church. The Orthodox weren’t involved, so they never had the Catholic–Protestant arguments.
  5. TodayOver 200 million Orthodox Christians, in self-governing churches (Greek, Russian, Serbian, Romanian, Antiochian and more) that share one faith.

“Orthodox” means “right belief” or “right worship”. Both meanings matter.

Five mindset shifts

  • Mystery is allowed

    Some things are beyond human explanation: the Trinity, how bread and wine become Christ’s Body and Blood. Orthodoxy says what’s true, then leaves the “how” as mystery. The sacraments are even called “the Mysteries”.

  • Worship comes first

    You learn the faith mostly by praying it, not by studying a system. An old saying: “A theologian is someone who prays truly.”

  • Salvation is healing

    Sin is seen less as a legal crime and more as a sickness. Christ is the doctor. The goal is theosis: being united with God and sharing his life (2 Peter 1:4).

  • The Church is a family

    It isn’t a group of individuals who happen to agree. It’s one Body across time, including those who have died in Christ. Bishops link it back to the apostles.

  • Your body is part of it

    You worship with your whole self: standing, bowing, crossing yourself, smelling incense, fasting, eating. Faith isn’t only in your head.

Tradition, not “traditions”. Orthodox talk a lot about Tradition. It means the Church’s living memory of the faith. The Bible sits at its centre. It’s not a pile of extra rules added on top.
